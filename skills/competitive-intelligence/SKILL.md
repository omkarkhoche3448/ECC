# Competitive Intelligence

Make decisions that grow the business, not slide decks that collect dust.

## When to Activate

- analyzing competitors or alternative products
- evaluating market positioning and differentiation
- making pricing or packaging decisions
- planning strategic moves (enter, pivot, defend, expand)
- assessing threats from new entrants or substitutes
- preparing for investor questions about competition
- deciding build vs buy vs partner
- evaluating acquisition targets or partnership opportunities

## Intelligence Standards

1. Analyze product reality, not marketing copy.
2. Separate what competitors say from what they do.
3. Every strategic recommendation must connect to revenue, cost, or defensibility.
4. Include the "so what" — what this means for your next move.
5. Time-stamp all intelligence. Markets shift fast.
6. Include contrarian evidence. If a competitor looks weak, find where they're strong.

## Analysis Frameworks

### 1. Competitor Deep Dive

For each competitor, collect:

**Product Reality**
- core features vs announced/beta
- actual user experience (not screenshots)
- technical architecture clues (job postings, tech blog, open source)
- integration ecosystem and API surface
- platform limitations and known pain points

**Business Signals**
- funding history, investors, and runway estimates
- revenue signals (pricing page, job growth, office expansion)
- customer count claims vs verifiable evidence
- churn signals (review trends, social sentiment, support forums)
- hiring patterns (what roles = what priorities)

**Go-to-Market**
- distribution channels (PLG, sales-led, partner-led)
- pricing model and packaging tiers
- target customer profile (SMB, mid-market, enterprise)
- content and SEO strategy
- community and developer relations

**Positioning**
- stated positioning vs perceived positioning
- key messaging and value props
- what they emphasize = what they think they win on
- what they avoid = where they're weak

### 2. Competitive Landscape Map

Plot competitors on two axes that matter for your market:

```
Axis options (pick two):
- Price vs Capability
- Ease of use vs Power
- Niche vs Platform
- Self-serve vs Sales-led
- AI-native vs AI-bolted-on
- Developer-first vs Business-user-first
```

Identify:
- crowded quadrants (red ocean)
- empty quadrants (opportunity or graveyard)
- your current position vs desired position
- movement direction of each competitor

### 3. SWOT That Actually Helps

Standard SWOT is useless without action items.

| | Helpful | Harmful |
|---|---|---|
| **Internal** | **Strengths** → How to leverage | **Weaknesses** → How to mitigate |
| **External** | **Opportunities** → How to capture | **Threats** → How to defend |

Each cell must include:
- the observation (fact-based)
- the implication (so what)
- the action (what to do about it)

### 4. Porter's Five Forces (Simplified)

Assess each force as HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW with evidence:

- **Rivalry intensity**: How hard do competitors fight? On what dimensions?
- **Buyer power**: Can customers easily switch? Do they have leverage?
- **Supplier power**: Do you depend on a platform, API, or vendor that could squeeze you?
- **New entrant threat**: How easy is it to build what you have? What's the moat?
- **Substitute threat**: What non-obvious alternatives solve the same job-to-be-done?

### 5. Strategic Move Analysis

For any strategic decision, evaluate:

**Offensive Moves**
- market expansion (new segments, geographies)
- product expansion (adjacent features, new use cases)
- pricing attacks (undercut, freemium, bundling)
- talent acquisition (hire from competitor)
- partnership plays (lock up distribution)

**Defensive Moves**
- switching cost creation (integrations, data gravity, workflow lock-in)
- speed of execution (ship faster than they can copy)
- community building (network effects, user-generated content)
- patent/IP protection (where applicable)
- strategic pricing (make their business model unviable)

**Timing Assessment**
- first mover advantage vs fast follower advantage
- market readiness signals
- resource requirements vs current capacity
- reversibility (can you undo this if wrong?)

### 6. Pricing Intelligence

Collect and analyze:
- competitor pricing pages (archive monthly — they change)
- actual deal prices (from reviews, forums, sales intel)
- packaging structure (what's gated at each tier)
- pricing model (per seat, usage, flat, hybrid)
- discount patterns (end of quarter, annual vs monthly)
- free tier limitations

Build a comparison matrix:

| Feature | You | Competitor A | Competitor B |
|---------|-----|-------------|-------------|
| Base price | | | |
| Per-seat cost | | | |
| Key limit | | | |
| Enterprise pricing | | | |

### 7. Win/Loss Analysis

For deals you won or lost:
- why the customer chose (their words, not your assumption)
- what alternatives they evaluated
- what almost made them choose differently
- price sensitivity vs feature sensitivity
- decision maker vs evaluator vs user priorities

## Profitability Decision Framework

For every strategic recommendation, answer:

1. **Revenue impact**: Will this increase revenue? By how much? How fast?
2. **Cost impact**: What does this cost to build, maintain, support?
3. **Margin impact**: Does this improve or compress margins?
4. **Defensibility impact**: Does this make the business harder to compete with?
5. **Opportunity cost**: What are we NOT doing if we do this?

Score each dimension: STRONG POSITIVE / POSITIVE / NEUTRAL / NEGATIVE / STRONG NEGATIVE

Only recommend moves that are net positive across these five dimensions. If a move is strong on revenue but negative on defensibility, flag the trade-off explicitly.

## Output Format

### Quick Competitive Brief (1-2 competitors)
1. Executive summary (3 sentences max)
2. Competitor snapshot table
3. Key differentiators (yours vs theirs)
4. Threats to monitor
5. Recommended action (1-2 moves)

### Full Competitive Analysis (market-wide)
1. Executive summary
2. Market landscape map
3. Competitor deep dives (per competitor)
4. SWOT with actions
5. Five forces assessment
6. Strategic recommendations (prioritized)
7. Pricing analysis
8. Risk register
9. Sources and data freshness

### Strategic Decision Brief
1. Decision context (what are we deciding)
2. Options evaluated (with pros/cons)
3. Competitive implications of each option
4. Profitability analysis
5. Recommendation with confidence level (HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW)
6. Reversibility assessment
7. Next steps and milestones

## Red Flags to Avoid

- copying competitor features without understanding why they built them
- assuming competitor weakness is permanent
- ignoring indirect competitors and substitutes
- pricing based only on competitors (price on value delivered)
- analysis paralysis — set a decision deadline
- confirmation bias — actively seek evidence against your thesis
- recency bias — one product launch doesn't change market dynamics

## Quality Gate

Before delivering:
- [ ] all claims are sourced or labeled as inference
- [ ] competitor data is recent (flag anything older than 6 months)
- [ ] every insight has an action attached
- [ ] profitability impact is quantified (even roughly)
- [ ] contrarian evidence is included
- [ ] recommendations are prioritized by impact and feasibility
- [ ] the output makes a business decision easier, not harder
